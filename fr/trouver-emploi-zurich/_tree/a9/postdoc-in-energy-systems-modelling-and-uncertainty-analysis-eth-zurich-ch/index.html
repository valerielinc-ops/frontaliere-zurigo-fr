Postdoc in Energy Systems Modelling and Uncertainty Analysis

100%, Zurich, fixed-term

At the Climate Policy Lab at ETH Zürich, we conduct problem-driven research on the strategies to address climate change and related environmental problems. We are part of the Institute for Environmental Decisions in the Department of Environmental Systems Science. Join us from 1 March 2027 (or by agreement) to lead our contribution to SURE-RES (Spatially-resolved uncertainty analysis for future renewable-based energy systems), a three-year project with EURAC Research in Bolzano, Italy, funded by the Swiss National Science Foundation.

This is a three-year position at 100%, starting on 1 March 2027 or by agreement, based in Zürich.

Project background

How much detail do energy system models need to support robust policy decisions? Detailed models are expensive to run, so uncertainty analyses usually rely on simplified ones, and we do not know how much these simplifications distort policy conclusions.

SURE-RES tackles this with a multi-fidelity approach. A high-resolution, sector-coupled PyPSA-Eur model of Europe (about 200 nodes, hourly resolution) serves as the reference. We compare two cheaper alternatives: spatially aggregated models combined with polynomial chaos expansion, and graph neural network surrogates developed by our colleagues at EURAC. We then apply the best approach to produce spatially resolved uncertainty assessments and policy guidance for Switzerland and South Tyrol.

Job description

You will lead ETH's contributions and work closely with the team at EURAC:

  • Coordination (WP1): coordinate the work across ETH and EURAC, organise project meetings and workshops, and manage reporting and open-source releases. The project is small, so coordination sits alongside your research.
  • High-fidelity model (WP2): build, calibrate, and validate the PyPSA-Eur model, define the uncertainty space, and generate the model runs on ETH's high-performance computing cluster.
  • Low-fidelity models (WP3): build, calibrate, and validate the low-fidelity models that reduce spatial resolution and use polynomial chaos expansion.
  • Comparison and policy translation (WP5): compare the low-fidelity approaches against the reference runs, work out which suits which planning question, and translate the results into policy recommendations.
  • Publish in peer-reviewed journals, present at conferences (e.g. ECEMP, IAEE, SDEWES) and release open-source code.
  • Co-supervise Bachelor's, Master's and doctoral researchers and teach in related courses.

  • Experience in energy systems modelling.
  • Solid Python for scientific computing skills, or similar stack.
  • Ability to work independently.
  • Interest in coordinating a project across two institutions.
  • Fluent written and spoken English.

Desirable

  • Experience with PyPSA, Calliope or similar frameworks.
  • Experience with high-performance computing.
  • Uncertainty or sensitivity analysis (e.g. Monte Carlo, Sobol indices, polynomial chaos expansion).
  • Familiarity with machine learning.
  • Experience communicating model results to policy-makers.
  • Working knowledge of German.

Candidates who do not possess all the listed skills are also encouraged to apply.
