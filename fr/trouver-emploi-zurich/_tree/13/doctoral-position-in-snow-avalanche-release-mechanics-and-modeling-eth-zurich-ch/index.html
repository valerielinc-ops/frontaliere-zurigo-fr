Doctoral Position in "Snow Avalanche Release Mechanics and Modeling"

100%, Zurich, fixed-term

The Chair of Alpine Mass Movements at ETH Zürich and SLF Davos is looking for a motivated Doctoral student to advance our understanding and prediction of snow avalanche release. You will connect fracture mechanics, numerical simulation, and observations to address a central challenge in avalanche hazard assessment: predicting how large an avalanche will be.

Project background

Why does a fracture sometimes stop after a few meters, while in other cases it spreads across an entire mountainside? Snow slab avalanches release through crack propagation in a buried weak layer, followed by fracture of the overlying slab. Their final size depends on how these processes interact with snowpack variability and terrain. Despite its importance for avalanche forecasting and hazard assessment, release size remains difficult to predict. Existing methods for identifying potential release areas rely mainly on topography and account only indirectly for snow mechanical properties.

Job description

  • You will investigate how crack propagation, crack arrest, and slab fracture determine avalanche release dimensions across complex mountain terrain
  • Using and further developing a depth-averaged Material Point Method established in our group, you will examine when release size is controlled by snowpack properties and when terrain becomes the dominant constraint
  • You will evaluate simulations against observations from instrumented slopes and avalanche release areas mapped from drone and satellite imagery
  • Building on this physical understanding, you will develop a probabilistic framework to assess how uncertain snow loading and mechanical properties affect the location and size of avalanche release zones
  • This will connect the mechanics of individual releases with release-area mapping at the mountain scale
  • As a subsequent application, you will couple release scenarios to our highly efficient depth-averaged flow solver to investigate how uncertainty in release conditions and friction parameters affects avalanche runout
  • You will publish your results in scientific journals, present your work at international conferences, and contribute to teaching activities at ETH Zürich

Profile

  • You hold an MSc in civil engineering, geotechnics, mechanics, geophysics, physics, computational science, or a related field
  • You have experience using or developing numerical models and strong programming skills
  • Experience in computational mechanics, probabilistic methods, or geospatial analysis would be useful
  • You are curious and enthusiastic about applying computational methods to snow avalanches and natural hazards
  • You must be fluent in English; knowledge of German or French is beneficial

We offer

Your Doctorate is part of the project “A multi-scale modeling framework for understanding and predicting snow avalanche release size”, funded by the Swiss National Science Foundation (SNSF). Under the supervision of Prof. Johan Gaume, you will collaborate with researchers in snow mechanics, avalanche forecasting, remote sensing and statistics.

You will join a diverse, international and multidisciplinary team, bringing together Doctoral students, postdoctoral researchers, scientists, engineers and support staff working on alpine mass movements and natural hazards. We value a supportive working atmosphere, open exchange of ideas and mutual respect. Regular social events and group activities provide opportunities to connect beyond our research.
