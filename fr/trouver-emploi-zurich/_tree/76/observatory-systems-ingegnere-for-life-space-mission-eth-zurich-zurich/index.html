Observatory Systems Engineer for LIFE Space Mission

80%-100%, Zurich, fixed-term

ETH Zurich | Space is dedicated to advancing space activities both within ETH and in collaboration with external stakeholders. Our mission is to position ETH Zurich as Europe's leading institution in space research and education, while also being recognized as a premier partner for space-related projects within Switzerland and globally. ETH Zurich | Space was founded in 2022 and has been led by Prof. Dr. Thomas Zurbuchen since 2023. We are committed to driving technological advancements, shaping future policies, and inspiring the next generation of space scientists and engineers.

Project background

The LIFE (Large Interferometer for Exoplanets) mission is an ETH Zurich-led concept for a space-based mid-infrared interferometer designed to characterize the atmospheres of nearby exoplanets and search for signs of habitability and life. As part of the ETH Zurich | Space engineering team, you will take a leading technical role in the development of LIFE, working closely with the mission's scientific and engineering teams, international partners, and industrial suppliers. Your work will primarily focus on prototype and precursor developments and, as the project evolves, may extend to elements of the full LIFE observatory. While LIFE will be your primary assignment, you may occasionally contribute to other ETH Zurich | Space missions. This position is offered as a fixed-term appointment for an initial period of two years, with the possibility of extension.

Job description

You will take senior technical responsibility for systems engineering of the LIFE observatory and its prototype and precursor developments, payload and spacecraft systems engineering, integration and test, and technical coordination. Working closely with the LIFE science team, mission leadership, international partners, and industrial suppliers, you will translate scientific objectives into technically feasible designs and lead their development and implementation across the participating organizations.

As part of your role, you will

  • Lead and coordinate the technical development of assigned LIFE mission, observatory, and payload elements across the mission consortium and industrial partners, ensuring alignment on requirements, interfaces, schedules, and technical decisions in close collaboration with LIFE project management.
  • Apply systems engineering practices and lessons learned from major space astrophysics missions, tailoring their implementation to the development and maturity of LIFE prototypes, precursor missions, and the full observatory.
  • Translate science goals into mission objectives, requirements, and mission-level concepts of operations with scientific teams.
  • Develop and manage requirements, specifications, and interface control documents, with requirements flowdown from mission to payload, instrument, and subsystem levels.
  • Lead payload and spacecraft systems engineering across multiple institutions and suppliers, defining and controlling interfaces between instruments, optical assemblies, structures, spacecraft platforms, and other subsystems.
  • Manage technical budgets for mass, power, thermal, data, pointing, and limited-life resources.
  • Develop mission architectures covering launch and orbit selection, platform and payload architecture, ground segment, operations planning, launch campaign preparation, and in-orbit commissioning.
  • Lead cross-discipline trade studies balancing scientific return, technology readiness, mission feasibility, schedule, and cost, particularly in early concept phases.
  • Plan and lead manufacturing, assembly, integration, and test activities for LIFE-related instruments, payloads, and observatory elements, coordinating multidisciplinary engineering and test teams across participating institutions and suppliers.
  • Develop and execute test plans and procedures from component to observatory level, including acceptance and post-delivery testing of externally supplied hardware and environmental testing (thermal vacuum, vibration, shock, EMI/EMC).
  • Manage nonconformances; support or coordinate failure and anomaly review boards, root-cause analysis, and corrective actions.
  • Lead mission, payload, and instrument verification and validation, maintaining verification traceability and requirements relevant to scientific objectives.
  • Lead technical risk identification, analysis, and mitigation using methods such as FMECA and fault tree analysis.
  • Lead the technical preparation and coordination of mission and payload lifecycle reviews, including defining review objectives, technical content, and required documentation, and presenting engineering results and recommendations at MDR, PRR, SRR, PDR, CDR, QR, AR, and test readiness reviews.
  • Provide technical oversight of suppliers developing flight and test hardware for LIFE, from specification through acceptance and delivery, and contribute to the integration and test infrastructure required for LIFE prototype, instrument, and observatory development.
  • Coordinate additional technical resources from students and external partners with the project leadership team, mentor junior engineers and students, and contribute to teaching in the Space Systems program where appropriate.
  • Contribute technical content to mission proposals and funding applications, including ESA, SSO, SNSF, and Innosuisse proposals.

Profile

You have extensive experience in space systems engineering and enjoy taking technical responsibility across disciplines and organizations. You combine sound engineering judgment with a practical approach to solving complex technical problems and coordinating multidisciplinary teams.

We are looking for someone with

  • A degree in aerospace engineering, mechanical engineering, electrical engineering, systems engineering, physics, or a related field, combined with substantial relevant professional experience. An advanced degree is an advantage.
  • 10+ years of relevant practical experience in space systems engineering, mission architecture, payload/instrument engineering, or spacecraft engineering.
  • Systems engineering experience at the integrated payload or observatory level on the James Webb Space Telescope, Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope, or a comparable large space astrophysics mission, ideally spanning element delivery through integration, test, launch and commissioning.
  • Experience serving as technical authority or interface lead for an integrated payload or observatory composed of elements from different institutions and suppliers (e.g., telescope assembly, science instruments, and supporting structure).
  • Demonstrated senior technical responsibility across the design, development, manufacturing, assembly, integration, and test lifecycle of space-flight missions, payloads, or instruments.
  • Hands-on experience leading verification and validation across multiple requirement levels (e.g., mission, payload, and element), and leading environmental test campaigns at payload or observatory level, including thermal vacuum, dynamics, and EMI/EMC.
  • Strong system-level analysis and trade-study skills, with an in-depth understanding of mission requirements, systems engineering principles, and lifecycle processes.
  • Experience managing technical requirements, interfaces, and supplier deliverables across multiple institutions and industrial partners, including technical oversight of flight-hardware subcontracts.
  • Experience with optical, infrared, or cryogenic science instruments, including cryogenic mechanisms, precision optical alignment, and their interfaces with telescope assemblies and observatory systems.
  • Experience leading anomaly resolution and failure review boards, including root-cause analysis, fault tree analysis, and corrective action.
  • Demonstrated technical leadership and sound engineering judgment, with the ability to establish priorities, resolve complex technical issues, and drive multidisciplinary engineering activities toward project milestones.
  • Proven ability to lead and coordinate distributed, multi-institution teams without direct line authority, building consensus among scientists, engineers, and industry partners while maintaining technical accountability.
  • Excellent command of written and spoken English. German is an advantage but not required.

Candidates who do not completely fulfill the mentioned skills are also encouraged to apply.

Additionally, the following characteristics are a plus

  • Experience in launch campaign, in-orbit commissioning, or mission operations planning for a space observatory.
  • Experience in early-stage mission development, concept studies, prototype missions, technology demonstrators, or small-satellite missions, ideally where requirements refinement reduced risk and cost and raised technology readiness.
  • Familiarity with ECSS, NASA, or comparable systems engineering, verification, product assurance, and review processes.
  • Awareness of the Swiss and European space ecosystem, including industry, academic institutions, and space agencies.

We offer

  • A dynamic, international, and interdisciplinary work environment at one of the leading technical universities in the world.
  • A motivated team of people sharing a passion for space and the vision to strengthen and enhance space research and activities at ETH Zurich.
  • An opportunity to help shape space missions from the very beginning.
