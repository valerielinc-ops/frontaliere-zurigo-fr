Doctoral Position in model-based optimization of industrial heat pumps

100%, Zurich, fixed-term

The Energy and Process Systems Engineering (EPSE) Group at ETH Zürich is looking for a doctoral student to work on a new generation of model-based process optimization methods for high-temperature industrial heat pumps.

The EPSE group, led by Prof. Dr. André Bardow, is dedicated to advancing sustainable energy and chemical process systems. Our research spans from the molecular level to the scale of systems. We develop simulation and experimental methods to advance sustainable energy and chemical processes from the molecular to the system scale.

Project background

Decarbonizing the industry is among the most pressing challenges of our time. Electrically driven heat pumps can reduce CO₂ emissions by more than 90% compared to conventional gas boilers when powered by green electricity, yet commercially available solutions reach only up to 160 °C, leaving the critical segment between 160 and 250 °C without viable options.

For this temperature range, the transcritical cycle, with evaporation below and gas cooling above the critical point, is thermodynamically superior to all current heat pump approaches. Transcritical heat pumps are ideally suited for applications where the heat sink undergoes large temperature changes of more than 60 K, a condition found in drying, pasteurization, pressurized water heating, and many other widespread industrial processes. Despite this clear thermodynamic advantage, no commercial transcritical heat pump exists for high-temperature applications. The barriers to commercialization are the absence of systematic cycle design tools, missing refrigerant-lubricant property data around the critical point, and untested component behavior in these conditions.

Job description

This project directly addresses these barriers through an integrated approach combining system-level modelling, property characterization, and experimental demonstration.

  • Your main task will be to advance superstructure-based process optimization models to identify the best cycle architectures and working fluids across industrial use cases defined with our industrial advisory board
  • The work will be conducted in tight integration with a companion doctoral candidate in our group, who will focus on advancing the experimental counterpart methods
  • The third (experimental) doctoral position envisaged by the project will be opened at the partner institution OST (Ost Schweizer Fachhochschule), the professors and senior researchers at both institutions will supervise the project.
  • Your role will also involve mentoring and co-supervising student projects and theses
  • Additionally, you will engage in various group and institute duties and activities
  • As an integral part of your work, you will publish your results in peer-reviewed journals and present them at international conferences

Profile

  • You meet the requirements for a doctoral program at ETH Zurich and have an excellent Master's or diploma in process engineering, mechanical engineering, energy science & technology, chemical engineering, or a related field.
  • Ideally, you already have experience in model-based process optimization methods
  • The ability to work independently and excellent communication and writing skills in English complete your profile

We offer a flexible and supportive working environment that fosters professional and personal growth . You will join a dynamic, motivated, and interdisciplinary team of researchers with expertise in thermodynamics, process design, energy system optimization, and life cycle assessment, working collaboratively with research and industry partners. You will work in an inspiring, collaborative environment to address critical global challenges. The position supports the development of critical thinking, data analysis, problem-solving, and project management skills while contributing to the broader academic community through publications and presentations at leading conferences.
